A founder asks for a "simple chatbot" and gets quotes from $500 to $50,000 for
the same three sentences of requirements. Both quotes can be correct. The
difference is what the chatbot has to know, where its answers come from, and
who fixes it at 2 a.m. when it starts making things up.

We build AI chatbots for clients, and we stopped hiding our numbers. This is
what an AI chatbot actually costs in 2026, which parts of the bill are worth
paying, and where you should not spend a rupee.

> **In short:** a template bot on your existing plan costs $500-$1,500. A custom
> bot trained on your business data costs $3,000-$15,000 to build and $100-$500
> per month to run. Anything above that is buying features you cannot name yet.

## What you are actually paying for

Every chatbot quote is made of the same four parts. When a price confuses you,
break it into these and ask which one is expensive.

**1. The brain (the language model).** Usually a monthly API bill paid directly
to OpenAI, Google, or Anthropic — $20 to $400 per month depending on how many
conversations you handle. This is almost never the big cost.

**2. The knowledge (your data).** Connecting the bot to your products, prices,
policies, and documents. This is the real work, and the part that separates a
toy from a tool.

**3. The plumbing (integrations).** WhatsApp, your website, your CRM, your
order system. Each connection is a separate piece of work.

**4. The babysitting (hosting and maintenance).** Updates, monitoring, fixing
wrong answers, retraining when your prices change.

## Price ranges you will actually see in 2026

### Tier 1: Template chatbot — $500 to $1,500

A bot built on an existing platform (Tidio, Chatbase, Botpress) pointed at your
website content. Live in days.

**Right choice when:** you want to answer the same 20 questions customers always
ask — hours, returns, delivery areas.

**Wrong choice when:** your business has rules a template cannot express, or
you need the bot to *do* things (check an order, book a slot), not just talk.

### Tier 2: Custom AI chatbot trained on your data — $3,000 to $15,000

The sweet spot for most small businesses. A bot that knows your catalog and
policies, speaks in your tone, lives on your website or WhatsApp, and hands
complex conversations to a human.

What moves the price inside this range:

- Number of integrations (website only is cheapest; WhatsApp + CRM + payments
  stacks up)
- Language support (English only, or English + Urdu + Arabic)
- Whether it must take actions — booking, ordering, checking status — instead
  of just answering
- How messy your source data is (a clean price list is cheap; 400 scanned PDFs
  are not)

### Tier 3: Custom platform or AI agent — $15,000 to $50,000+

Multi-department systems where the AI executes workflows: processes invoices,
chases leads, writes reports. This is AI automation territory, not a chatbot
any more. If a vendor quotes you this for a customer-support bot, ask them to
explain which of the four parts above justifies it.

## The monthly bill nobody quotes you upfront

The build cost is one-time. These repeat:

- **Model API usage:** $20-$400/month (grows with traffic)
- **Hosting:** $10-$100/month
- **Maintenance contract:** $100-$500/month — keeps answers correct when your
  prices, policies, or products change

A chatbot with no maintenance budget fails quietly: it starts giving outdated
answers, customers stop trusting it, and by the time anyone notices, it has
cost more than it saved.

## Five ways to cut the cost without cutting quality

1. **Start with your top 30 questions.** Write down what customers actually ask
   (WhatsApp history is gold). Scope to that. Add the rest in month three.
2. **One channel first.** Website OR WhatsApp. Not both on day one.
3. **Give the bot clean data.** A tidy FAQ document can cut days off the build.
4. **Skip the avatar and small talk.** Personality is cheap; correctness is not.
5. **Ask what happens when the bot does not know.** A good bot escalates to a
   human gracefully. Building that in from day one is cheaper than bolting it
   on after a bad launch.

## How to tell a serious quote from a bad one

Ask any vendor these five questions. The answers separate engineers from
middlemen:

- What happens when the bot is asked something not in its data?
- Which model will you use, and why that one over the alternatives?
- Who pays the API bill, and what does it cost at 1,000 conversations a month?
- Show me a bot you built that is still live today.
- What does maintenance include, and what happens if I stop paying for it?

If the answers are vague, the price is not real — it is a guess with a logo on
it.

## What we would tell you on a call

If you just need the same 20 questions answered, take the template route and
spend the savings on ads. If your customers ask things tied to *your* catalog,
prices, or policies — or you want the bot to actually do work — a custom build
pays for itself within the year, and the monthly run cost is smaller than one
support hire.

We publish our pricing logic because we would rather win the project than the
quote. If you want a real number for your case, [tell us what the bot should
do](/contact) and we will reply with a fixed price, not a range.
